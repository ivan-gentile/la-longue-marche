**AI-assisted transcription of Grothendieck manuscripts (with Mateo
Carmona, CSG)**

Since early 2026 I have been collaborating with Mateo Carmona on
AI-assisted transcription of Grothendieck's unpublished writings. The
work has two complementary aims, agreed with the CSG archivist.

The first is practical: producing preliminary LaTeX transcription
drafts of comparatively legible documents — so far *La Longue Marche à
travers la théorie de Galois* Part II (Cote 140-3/140-4, 976 pages),
the Bourbaki *Schémas* typescript ("Préschémas", 437 pages), and
*Catégories de variétés* (n° 262, 100 pages) — to facilitate access to
the material and reduce the routine part of preparing research
resources. These drafts are explicitly non-authoritative: every page
carries machine-written position markers aligned to the source scans, a
page-level coverage manifest declares exactly what is and is not
transcribed, and a structured anomaly-reporting channel feeds the
archivist's line-by-line verification, which remains the central and
authoritative step.

The second aim is methodological: developing and rigorously evaluating
transcription systems for Grothendieck's difficult handwritten
material. Using Section 49 of *La Longue Marche* as the representative
test case, we measure both style conformance and content fidelity
(omissions, silent normalisations, insertions) against the archivist's
corrected text, and no pipeline change ships unless it improves those
measurements. As the methodology matures we intend to document it as a
resource for the broader scholarly community, in coordination with the
CSG.

All code, evaluation data and documentation are public at
https://github.com/ivan-gentile/la-longue-marche. Fidelity to the
original documents is the project's governing standard, and nothing
implying CSG involvement or representation is published without prior
agreement with the Centre.

I am a data scientist (ML/AI) and would contribute this work to the CSG
as a volunteer, under whatever repository privileges and review
processes the Centre considers appropriate.
